# Leopard Seal: The Hunter at the Edge of the Ice

## Cold Open
[SHOT: A crowd of penguins standing at the edge of the sea ice above dark water]
A crowd of penguins stands at the edge of the ice. They're hungry, and the fish are right there, below them. But not one of them moves.

[SHOT: The dark water under the ice edge, still and quiet]
They're waiting for someone else to go first. Because somewhere in that dark water, something may be waiting too.

[SHOT: A leopard seal gliding underwater]
Meet the leopard seal, the top hunter of the Antarctic coast, and one of the strangest predators on the planet.

## Built Like A Hunter
[SHOT: The seal beside a human silhouette for scale]
Leopard seals are long and lean, with a huge head that looks almost reptilian. The biggest reach around 3.5 meters, about 11 feet, and can weigh close to half a ton. [VERIFY: maximum length and weight] And here's a twist: the biggest leopard seals are the females. [VERIFY: females larger than males]

[SHOT: Close view of the spotted coat]
They're named for the dark spots on their silver-gray coat, like a big cat's.

## Two Mouths In One
[SHOT: The seal's head with its jaws open]
Open that enormous mouth and you'll find two toolkits. At the front, long canines for grabbing prey. At the back, cheek teeth with three points each that lock together when the jaws close, like a kitchen strainer. [VERIFY: three-pointed cheek teeth]

[SHOT: A swarm of krill drifting in blue water]
That lets the seal take in a mouthful of krill, tiny shrimp-like animals, and push the water back out. So a penguin hunter can also feed a little like a whale. [VERIFY: krill in the diet]

## Ambush At The Ice Edge
[SHOT: The seal waiting just below the surface near the ice]
The leopard seal's favorite weapon is patience. It lingers close to the ice edge, near the spots where penguins enter and leave the water. [VERIFY: ice-edge ambush]

[SHOT: Penguins diving in together while the seal turns]
When the penguins finally jump, they often go together. A crowd is harder to target than a lone bird. [VERIFY: group entry]

[SHOT: Other prey: fish and a young seal]
Penguins aren't the only prey. Leopard seals also eat fish, squid and the young of other seal species. [VERIFY: diet]

## Meeting Humans
[SHOT: A diver underwater with a seal circling at a distance]
Most leopard seals that meet divers are simply curious. They circle, watch, open their mouths, and swim away. [VERIFY: most encounters are curiosity]

[SHOT: The seal pushing a penguin toward a diver]
One wildlife photographer described a female that kept bringing him penguins, as if she was trying to feed him. [VERIFY: Paul Nicklen encounter]

[SHOT: A quiet Antarctic research station at dusk]
But they are powerful wild predators. In 2003, a leopard seal killed British marine biologist Kirsty Brown while she was snorkeling near her research station. It's the only known human death caused by the species, and it changed how scientists there work in the water. [VERIFY: 2003 incident and its effect on safety rules]

## A Changing Ice
[SHOT: Sea ice breaking apart]
Leopard seals rest, give birth and molt on the sea ice. As the Antarctic's ice changes, scientists are watching closely to see what that means for this hunter. [VERIFY: reliance on sea ice]

## Outro
[SHOT: Penguins at the edge again, one finally jumps]
So the next time you see penguins hesitating at the edge of the ice, you'll know why. They're not being shy. They're being smart.
